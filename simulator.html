Strategy tester — automated simulated trades

Strategy rules are replayed over official Kalshi demo history and the resulting simulated trades are scored against each market's own settlement value. Nothing is estimated and nothing is invented: every price comes from a candlestick the API returned, and every outcome comes from the settlement_value_dollars field of a finalized market.

Simulation assumptions — read before reading the numbers

  • A signal is filled at the trade-close price of the candle that fired it. No spread and no slippage are applied, so results are still optimistic. Trading fees are modelled from the official schedule: taker fee = round up(M × 0.07 × C × P × (1−P)) with the exchange's own fee-rounding mechanics (trade fee rounded up to $0.000001, then a rounding fee aligns the balance change to the member's precision grid). Both a fee-per-contract column and a P&L-after-fees column are shown below. Sources: fee schedule (PDF) ↗ · fee rounding ↗. Settlement exits pay no settlement fee (the schedule states there is none); round-trip exits pay a taker fee on the exit fill. Series listed in the schedule's Non-Standard Fees table with a taker multiplier of 0 pay no fee.
  • One trade per market per strategy, taken at the first signal.
  • A position held to settlement pays the market's own settlement_value_dollars — 1.0000 for YES, 0.0000 for NO; the NO contract entry price is calculated as 1 − the YES quote (market settlement ↗).
  • The seeded random-control baseline chooses one pseudo-random candle and side per market. It is reproducible for the same market ticker/history, not a forecast, and is not evidence of statistical significance.
  • The demo exchange holds mock funds and its prices may not match production (demo environment ↗).

Strategy scoreboard

One row per strategy. Hit rate is the share of scored trades that made money; avg entry price is what the market was charging when the signal fired. Reading the two side by side is the point: a hit rate above the average entry price means the signal did better than the price it paid. A strategy with no trades earns nothing and is reported as such rather than hidden.

Live paper trading

The same automation, running against live demo prices. When a market in the five topics swings by at least $0.10 and clears the tradeability screen, a simulated position is opened on the side that moved, at the price the API last reported. Positions are held until the market settles, then scored with the market's own settlement value.

These trades are simulated only — nothing is sent to the exchange, no key is needed, and the book is stored in this browser's local storage. It is the automated version of the Portfolio page, which places real demo orders with your own key.
